# Hit Zero ARCADE — Build Report

July 3, 2026 · for Andrew (and anyone he shows it to)

## What got built

In one working day, Hit Zero gained its fourth spoke: a place athletes *want* to open the app.
The ARCADE is a Roblox-style shared world where the only people inside are a kid's real
teammates — live in production at thehitzero.net, on for Magic City athletes today.

Two connected worlds:

**The Clubhouse (lobby).** A stylized cheer-gym hangout: spring floor in the gym's own colors,
pennants, trophies, a banner with the gym's name pulled live from the database. Three arcade
cabinets line the back wall — two glow "COMING SOON" in attract mode, and the center one is a
working portal. Kids walk around with a touch joystick, flip automatically on the tumble track,
blast the spirit megaphone, and take posed photos in the photo booth.

**Cheer Town (through the center cabinet).** A little Main Street at dusk with the kid's own gym
as the centerpiece — her program's name in rooftop lights. Five pastel houses with doorbells, a
park with a practice mat, streetlights, a stop sign, and a golf cart she can actually drive.
Walk through the gym's glowing doors and there's a full pretend competition inside: spring floor,
a judges' table with three judges, a star on the mat. Stand on it, tap, and her avatar throws a
routine — spirit fingers, toe touch, backflip, HIT — then the scores pop up (sometimes a 10!),
confetti flies, and there's a podium for the champion moment.

**The people.** Every athlete gets a chibi cheerleader avatar. The first time she ever opens the
Arcade, a "Build your cheerleader!" screen appears — skin tone, hair, bow, uniform — and every
tap saves instantly, forever, on any device. Teammates appear live with name tags, moving
smoothly, doing emotes (HIT!, toe touch, backflip, heart hands…) and preset phrases ("HIT ZERO!",
"Watch this!", "See you at practice!"). Three **Super Squad** NPCs — superhero cheerleaders in
capes — wander the worlds throwing skills the girls can't do yet (full twists, doubles, sky-high
jumps) and being relentlessly generous: they cheer kids on, celebrate their emotes with confetti,
and a high-five gifts a 45-second sparkle trail. A persistent minimap in the corner shows the
map, teammates, and the Super Squad at all times.

## The safety story (the pitch to parents and owners)

- The social graph **is the roster**. The only way in is an athlete login at a real gym; the
  server (not the app) enforces that a gym's room only admits that gym's members.
- **No free text exists anywhere.** Communication is eight emotes and eight preset phrases.
  No DMs, no voice, no images, no strangers — structurally, not by moderation.
- **Nothing is recorded.** Movement and phrases evaporate in transit; the only thing stored is
  each kid's avatar choices.
- Coaches and owners can enter as observers, and when they do, every athlete sees a visible
  "👀 Coach is here" tag — transparency, not surveillance. No purchases anywhere.

## How it was built (process)

The day followed the approved plan (docs/ARCADE_BUILD_PLAN.md) phase by phase, with a
verify-before-ship loop on every drop: build → run in a real browser → walk the world, tap every
interactable, measure framerate, check the console → commit → deploy → verify production → repeat.
Six production releases shipped (hz-v79 → v83): scaffold + world + multiplayer + social layer,
then the first-run avatar builder (Andrew's call), a realtime-connection hardening pass after
Andrew personally hit a cold-start bug, then Cheer Town, then the Super Squad + minimap.

Notable bugs caught and fixed *before* kids saw them: a camera-centering bug from a PixiJS v8
API subtlety, buildings being painted over by the ground (layer-ordering), the player's own
avatar swallowing taps on things she stood on, and the emote wheel clipping off narrow screens.
The one bug a user (Andrew) did hit — a stuck "Reconnecting…" on the project's first-ever
realtime connection — now has self-healing reconnect logic and diagnostic logging behind it.

## The foundation (why the next things are cheap)

The engine was built as a platform, not a one-off:

- **No build step, no big downloads.** Plain JavaScript + PixiJS (WebGL). All art and sound are
  generated by code — crisp on retina iPads, instantly re-themed to any gym's colors, ~80KB of
  game code total. White-labeling a new gym is automatic: name, colors, banner, rooftop sign all
  flow from the database row.
- **Worlds are plug-ins.** A map is one file: layout, collision, decorations, interactables.
  Cheer Town was built on the exact engine the lobby proved out; the next world (house
  interiors, a rhythm game, a comp-weekend event space) is the same shape of work.
- **Abilities are gated by design.** The Super Squad's advanced skills already exist in every
  avatar — athletes just can't trigger them yet. "Land your full IRL → unlock it in the Arcade"
  is a config change wired to the existing skill-mastery hook, not a new system. Capes shipped
  as the first cosmetic slot for exactly this.
- **Multiplayer scales per gym.** Each gym is its own private channel; movement costs no
  database writes. Two athletes or thirty, the architecture is the same.
- Performance headroom: 121fps measured in testing against a 60fps target on the primary device.
